IV.
Charter

Why nobody can cheat this — and what it still can't tell you

The strength of this registry is what it lacks. The numbers on the right are read from the contract right now, not written into this page.

No owner. No admin. No upgrade path.

The contract has no owner or admin role, no pause switch, no way to change fees and no upgrade mechanism. The only things its deployer chose are five numbers, fixed at deployment and shown here. After that the deployer has exactly the powers of any stranger.

Append-only history

A correction adds version N+1 and links to N. Earlier versions are never edited or deleted, so “the provider said X on this date and only fixed it to Y later” is provable.

The money rule is in the code

Each payment is split between provider and platform at the moment it is made, then collected by withdrawal. A provider wallet that refuses money cannot block anyone else's subscription.

What it does not tell you

  • That the data is true. A provider can register hashes of bad data; the registry proves what was claimed and when.
  • That a declared data date is real. Only the block timestamp is trustworthy, and block time can drift by a few seconds.
  • That a subscriber received data. Delivery happens off-chain; “access” is only an on-chain signal.
  • That a simple row can't be guessed from its hash, if the row is easy to guess.
  • Anything about refunds (there are none) or lost keys (unrecoverable).
